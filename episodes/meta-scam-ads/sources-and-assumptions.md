# Evidence map and scope

Research checked 23 October 2026. The episode distinguishes reported allegations, Meta’s position, company-reported enforcement data, regulator data, and original fictional reconstruction. Legal status is stated as of that date, not presented as a continuously updated docket.

## Case coverage

[Reuters, Jeff Horwitz and Katie Paul, 22 October 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/rhode-island-sues-meta-over-alleged-tolerance-scam-advertising-2026-10-22/). [Accessible Reuters syndication](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiverhode-island-sues-meta-over-alleged-tolerance-for-scam-advertising-4967008), updated 15:36 that day.

Scenes 2–3: Rhode Island’s civil suit alleges profiting from scam ads and participating in creation/optimization; requested remedies include penalties, restitution and practice changes. These are allegations and requested relief, not findings or an awarded refund. Scene 4: Reuters reports Meta calls the case unfounded and intends to fight it. Scene 6: Reuters describes the complaint’s example of impersonating a bank executive and directing people toward bogus stock trades on WhatsApp. The real executive is not portrayed as endorsing the scheme. Scenes 17 and 23 retain disputed status.

The original Rhode Island complaint was not independently obtained in this production. The case summary is attributed to Reuters reporting; no claim is made to have reviewed the full court file. No case number, promised litigation outcome, criminal conviction or future refund entitlement is invented. No financial estimate from the Reuters internal-document investigation is repeated.

## Meta’s position and removal count

[Meta Newsroom: Fighting Scammers and Protecting People With New Technology and Partnerships](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/fighting-scammers-protecting-people-with-new-technology-and-partnerships/), originally 11 March 2026, updated in March.

Scenes 4–5 and 17: Meta says it removed more than 159 million scam ads in 2025 and is expanding advertiser verification and anti-scam technology. This is a company-reported count, not an independently audited detection rate, not a user/victim count and not proof of the safety of a particular ad. The count alone supplies no total-scam-ad denominator. The episode does not claim that Meta has no safeguards or approves every ad manually.

## Social-media-origin losses

[FTC: How to spot the top scams that started on social media](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/04/how-spot-top-scams-started-social-media), April 2026.

Scenes 15–16: people reported losing $2.1 billion in 2025 to scams that started on social media. This is reported loss data covering multiple platforms and forms of contact, not Meta-only or paid-ad-only losses. It is not a liability finding. It must not be divided by Meta’s ad-removal count, because the units, coverage and reporting mechanisms differ. No estimate of total unreported loss is made.

## Reconstruction and verification

[California DOJ: Investment scams on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-warns-californians-investment-scams-facebook-instagram), 6 April 2026. Scenes 9 and 11: deceptive/AI impersonations, messaging-app handoffs, false testimonials and fraudulent investment platforms. The advisory describes multiple scam patterns; the episode’s fictional journey is not presented as one documented victim’s experience or every investment group’s behavior.

[SEC / Investor.gov: Social Media and Investment Fraud](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/social-media-and-investment-fraud-investor-alert), 29 August 2022. Scenes 10 and 18–19: testimonials and celebrity images are unreliable evidence; independently research identities, recommendations and opportunities. A logo, visual polish, ad placement or active discussion is not a guarantee. The video does not promise that superficial AI artifact detection can establish authenticity.

[FTC: Investment scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/investment-scams). Scenes 12–13: fake investing sites and profit displays. [California DOJ advisory](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-warns-californians-investment-scams-facebook-instagram) also describes demands for taxes/fees when attempting withdrawal. The depicted $500–$825 balance is invented, not a return forecast or actual trading data.

[FTC: What To Do if You Were Scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed). Scene 20: prompt contact with the bank/payment service and saving records; [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) is the official FTC reporting destination. Refund/reversal/recovery is not guaranteed; procedures depend on payment method and facts.

[FTC: Refund and Recovery Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams). Scene 21: unsolicited promises to recover losses for an upfront fee can be a second scam.

Alex, the gray-haired presenter, the investment-club ad, comments, chat messages, account dashboard and balances are original fictional illustrations. The presenter is not an identifiable celebrity or the bank executive in the reported allegation. The domain `example.invalid` is reserved for examples.
