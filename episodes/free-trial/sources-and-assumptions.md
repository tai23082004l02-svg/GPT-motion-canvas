# Sources and editorial scope

Checked 23 October 2026. This is a general explainer for US viewers, not instructions for one particular provider or a statement of universal refund rights.

- [FTC: Getting In and Out of Free Trials, Auto-Renewals, and Negative Option Subscriptions](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions): recurring billing after trials, checking terms, reminders, official cancellation instructions, confirmation records and monitoring statements. Scenes 5, 7, 18, 20 and closing practical framing. This does not establish that every trial is deceptive.
- [FTC: Bringing Dark Patterns to Light (2022)](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light): cancellation obstacles and interface choices that undermine intended decisions. Scenes 8–14 are an original dramatization of design patterns. No prevalence or causal effect-size statistic is asserted.
- [Apple: Cancel a subscription](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428): subscriptions purchased through Apple, identifying the billing company/account from receipts and statements. Scenes 15 and 17. Apple guidance currently recommends canceling unwanted trial renewals at least 24 hours before the trial ends; other providers have different terms. The narration deliberately avoids one universal cutoff.
- [Google Play: Cancel, pause, or change a subscription](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481?hl=en): uninstalling does not cancel a Play subscription; use the correct account and complete cancellation instructions. Scenes 15–17. Google distinguishes canceling future renewal from refunds and payment-plan obligations. No suggestion to evade an existing payment commitment.
- [Spotify: How to cancel Premium plans](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/cancel-premium/): canceling a zero-priced free trial switches the account to free immediately; ordinary Premium generally continues to the next billing date. Scene 19 uses this as evidence that access outcomes depend on the terms, without depicting Spotify or generalizing its policy to all services.
- [CFPB: How do I dispute a charge on my credit card bill?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-dispute-a-charge-on-my-credit-card-bill-en-61/): prompt issuer contact and written dispute procedures. Scene 22. The video is not a complete dispute-rights guide. US credit-card billing-error protection can require written notice within 60 calendar days after a statement; debit rules and individual cases differ. The script promises no refund or chargeback outcome.

## Fiction and boundaries

Alex, FRAME, the maze, Example Store, the 7-day trial, $19.99 renewal, discount, messages, dates and button designs are illustrative. No service screenshots, logos, or specific-company allegations are used. Timing and prices are not market averages. Forgetting and interruption are character events, not psychological effect estimates. Some verification and confirmation steps serve legitimate purposes, which the script expressly recognizes.

The episode makes no claim that the FTC 2024 click-to-cancel rule is currently in effect, nor a claim that every subscription has identical legal cancellation requirements. The story separates cancellation of future renewals, access to remaining trial days, and refund eligibility. Keeping a useful subscription remains a valid choice.
