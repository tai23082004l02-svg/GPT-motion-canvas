The $60,000 income and $100,000 worker-year cost are **invented round-number assumptions**, not US median-income data or quoted hiring costs. Salary comparisons ignore living costs, taxes, investment returns, inflation and changes in pay. Spending-duration comparisons assume all wealth begins as cash and ignore returns, inflation, taxes, fees and valuation changes. The phone comparison also ignores supply, bulk purchasing effects, liquidation effects and transaction costs.

## Financial explanations

- Net worth: [SEC Investor.gov — Figure Out Your Finances](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/figure-out-your-finances) and [US Census — Wealth and Asset Ownership FAQ](https://www.census.gov/topics/income-poverty/wealth/about/faq.html). Assets less liabilities; example numbers are original.
- Ownership/voting: [Investor.gov — Stock](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/stock). Rights depend on shares and governance; the script does not state Musk’s voting percentages or the powers of a specific corporate office.
- Valuation formula: [Investor.gov — Market Capitalization](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/market-capitalization). The fictional company is not Tesla, SpaceX or a reconstruction of a Musk transaction.
- Liquidity: [FINRA — Understanding Disclosure Documents](https://syndication.finra.org/content/understanding-disclosure-documents). A readily quoted price is not a guaranteed execution price for an entire large holding. No quantitative market-impact model is invented.
- Secured borrowing: [Fidelity — Understanding the benefits and risks of margin](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/understanding-benefits-risks-margin), [SEC — Margin: Borrowing Money to Pay for Stocks](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/margin.htm) and [Investor.gov — Margin Call](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/margin-call). General illustrative mechanism only; the episode does not allege a particular Musk loan, tax strategy or private bank balance. Loan terms vary; margin/securities-backed loans are not interchangeable with every form of borrowing.
- GDP: [BEA — Gross Domestic Product](https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product) and [IMF — GDP explainer](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/gross-domestic-product-gdp). Production over a period versus assets/liabilities at a point in time. No country is assigned a sale price, and no numerical country-GDP comparison is used.

## Editorial guardrails

- Narrate **“equivalent to” / “on paper”**, not an unqualified claim of an executable 770-million-phone purchase.
- Date the wealth figure visibly near its first appearance. Show $1,199 and the exact Pro model; phone screens/hands may be simplified drawings.
- The cashier, store, household, toy company and project are fictional illustrations. No quote is attributed to Musk; use the ordinary Michael narrator, not an imitation.
- Do not depict a valuation gain as money pouring directly into a personal checking account without the correction in the script.
- Do not imply illiquidity makes the fortune fictitious or eliminates the owner’s financial options.
- Do not convert unrealized gains to a purported hourly salary. Do not call a national GDP a country’s purchase price.
- The labor-budget illustration purchases no guaranteed outcome and covers only its stated assumed labor budget. It is not a price quotation for schools, healthcare or housing.
