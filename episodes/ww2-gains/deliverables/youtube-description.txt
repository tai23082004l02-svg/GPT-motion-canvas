Who gained the most after World War II — and what does “gained” actually mean?

In 1945, military victory, economic strength, and everyday prosperity did not line up neatly. This illustrated history explainer follows the United States, Soviet Union, Canada, Australia, West Germany, and Japan through the early postwar years and the decades that followed.

We compare industrial capacity, geopolitical influence, and economic recovery — then widen the question to independence and sovereignty. This is a comparison of selected cases, not a definitive ranking of every country or an argument that war created a net benefit for humanity.
