# Research and editorial scope

Researched 24 October 2026. This is a historical explainer about relative postwar positions and subsequent recovery, not a claim that war was beneficial overall. Six selected cases are compared; this is not an exhaustive ranking. Economic capacity, geopolitical influence, household outcomes, and sovereignty are separate criteria. Immediate/early-postwar outcomes are distinguished from developments in the 1950s–1960s.

All narration is original synthesis. Workshop, house and table analogies are explanatory inventions. Characters and settings are illustrative composites, not depictions of documented individual encounters. No invented GDP totals, quantitative growth charts, or additive “benefit scores” are used.

## Evidence by chapter

### United States — industry and relative position

- [US National Park Service, Mobilizing a Nation for War](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/worldwarii/mobilization.htm): industrial mobilization and leading industrial-producer position by 1945.
- [US Department of Defense, Industries Transitioned From Peacetime to Wartime Production](https://www.defense.gov/Explore/Features/Story/Article/2128446/during-wwii-industries-transitioned-from-peacetime-to-wartime-production/): production, conversion and mobilization. The episode avoids converting a wartime-production share into an unsupported share of world GDP.
- [NIST, Postwar Years: Overview](https://www.nist.gov/pao/nist-100-foundations-progress/postwar-years-overview): inflation, strikes, shortages and the civilian transition.
- [National WWII Museum, The Cost of Victory](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/cost-victory): devastation, displacement and American relative economic dominance. Its sweeping statement about all other industrial nations is **not** repeated; Canada, Australia and neutral states make that formulation too broad.
- [National WWII Museum, Explore WWII History](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/students-teachers/student-resources/explore-wwii-history): military/human context and Japanese American incarceration.

Wording is limited to the main continental industrial centers avoiding sustained destruction. It does not assert that no US territory was attacked: Pearl Harbor/Hawaii, Alaska and other attacks are not erased by the comparison. “Clearest overall beneficiary” is an editorial conclusion under the explicitly stated criteria, not a historical statistic or a claim about every citizen.

### United States — monetary institutions, aid, partnerships

- [Federal Reserve History, Creation of the Bretton Woods System](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/bretton-woods-created): 44 countries, July 1944; fixed but adjustable exchange rates; dollar/gold link at $35 per ounce; institutional chronology.
- [US Office of the Historian, Bretton Woods](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1937-1945/bretton-woods): IMF and International Bank for Reconstruction and Development/World Bank origins and the monetary framework.
- [US National Archives, Marshall Plan (1948)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/marshall-plan): proposal in 1947, enabling act in 1948, reconstruction purpose.
- [Office of the Historian, Marshall Plan](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/marshall-plan): recovery and strategic context.

The Bretton Woods mechanism is labeled historical; the episode does not imply present-day gold convertibility. Assistance is neither described as pure altruism nor reduced to a simple purchase of political obedience.

### Soviet Union — power, sovereignty and costs

- [Office of the Historian, Wartime Conferences](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1937-1945/war-time-conferences): military position, political leverage, Eastern Europe and territorial concessions.
- [Office of the Historian, Potsdam Conference](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1937-1945/potsdam-conf): occupation, territorial settlements, Polish border changes and forced movements.
- [Office of the Historian, US–Soviet Alliance, 1941–1945](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1937-1945/us-soviet): alliance, territory taken during 1939–1940, Baltic incorporation and non-recognition.
- [Office of the Historian, Early Cold War foreword](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/foreword): Soviet domination of Eastern Europe and emerging superpower confrontation.
- [National WWII Museum, History Through the Viewfinder](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/history-through-viewfinder-41): mortality estimates vary, approximately 23–27 million. The script conservatively says “tens of millions” and includes civilians and military.
- [National WWII Museum, The Cost of Victory](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/cost-victory): Soviet destruction, loss and reconstruction.

Soviet Union is not used as a synonym for modern Russia. Satellite influence is distinguished from annexation. Baltic incorporation was during the war, not newly initiated in 1945, and non-recognition is stated. State power is not added to human loss to create a net welfare score.

### Canada

- [Canadian War Museum, A Nation Transformed](https://www.warmuseum.ca/cwm/exhibitions/chrono/1931transformed_e.html): industrial transformation, full employment by 1942 and assertive international role.
- [Statistics Canada, Canada at War](https://www65.statcan.gc.ca/acyb01/acyb01_0010-eng.htm): wartime production, employment, government spending and industrial change.
- [Veterans Affairs Canada, Canada’s Industries Gear Up for War](https://veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/classroom-materials/lesson-plans/historical-sheets/canadas-industries-gear-war): production and conversion back to a civilian economy.
- [NATO, Canada and NATO](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/nato-history/history-by-theme/my-country-and-nato/canada-and-nato): founding membership in 1949.
- [Canadian War Museum, Canada and the Second World War](https://www.warmuseum.ca/in-their-own-voices/learning-tools/canada-and-the-second-world-war/): campaigns, home-front changes and the uneven experience of war.
- [Canadian War Museum, Forced Relocation](https://www.warmuseum.ca/cwm/exhibitions/chrono/1931forced_e.html): forced displacement and dispossession. Characters do not imply that every Canadian shared equally in industrial gains.

### Australia

- [Australian Bureau of Statistics, Year Book Australia 1988 historical overview](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs%40.NSF/Previousproducts/1301.0Feature%20Article161988?issue=1988&num=&opendocument=&prodno=1301.0&tabname=Summary&view=): wartime manufacturing and postwar investment, immigration and infrastructure.
- [National Museum of Australia, Postwar Immigration Drive](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/postwar-immigration-drive): 1945 Immigration Department, migration and discriminatory restrictions.
- [National Archives of Australia, Ben Chifley: During Office](https://www.naa.gov.au/explore-collection/australias-prime-ministers/ben-chifley/during-office): civilian transition, demand for workers, housing and investment.
- [National Museum of Australia, Snowy Mountains Hydro](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/snowy-mountains-hydro): construction began in 1949, international labor and infrastructure. No fabricated power-output statistic is shown.
- [Office of the Historian, ANZUS](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/anzus): shift in security concerns and treaty; signed 1951, effective 1952.

Australia is not depicted as untouched by attack. Its development is attributed to postwar choices and resources, not to victory alone. The Snowy project is a representative case, not a claim that one project explains all national growth.

### West Germany

- [German Federal Government, Basic Law](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-en/federal-government/basic-law-for-the-federal-republic-of-germany-482266): founding constitutional chronology, May 1949.

- [Deutsche Bundesbank, Economic and Currency Reform of 1948](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/press/contributions/the-economic-and-currency-reform-of-1948-the-basis-for-stable-money-915302): western-zone currency reform, price controls, confidence, harsh effects on savers and uneven transition.
- [Deutsche Bundesbank, Währungsreform 1948](https://www.bundesbank.de/de/aufgaben/themen/waehrungsreform-1948-614040): imports and role of Marshall assistance.
- [German Federal Economics Ministry, Economic Policy Since 1949](https://www.bmwk.de/Redaktion/DE/Textsammlungen/Ministerium/wirtschaftspolitik-seit-1949.html): reforms, assistance, demand and recovery.
- [European Union, History 1945–1959](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/history-eu/1945-59_en): coal and steel cooperation and Treaty of Rome.
- [European Parliament, First Treaties](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/sheet/1/the-first-treaties): ECSC signed 1951/effective 1952, EEC treaties signed 1957/effective 1958.
- [Office of the Historian, Berlin Airlift](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/berlin-airlift): western zones and the developing East/West division.

The case is the Federal Republic/West Germany, not all of postwar Germany. Currency reform occurred in the western occupation zones before the Federal Republic was established in 1949. The title band’s “long-run recovery” denotes the section’s scope, not a claim that every event in it occurred after 1950. No exact percentage contribution is assigned to aid or any individual policy.

### Japan

- [National Diet Library of Japan, Reconstruction Outline](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha5/index.html): defeat, occupation and political reconstruction.
- [Office of the Historian, Occupation and Reconstruction of Japan 1945–52](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/japan-reconstruction): reforms, occupation stages and strategic shift.
- [Office of the Historian, Korean War and Japan’s Recovery](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/korean-war): Cold War and reconstruction context.
- [Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Diplomatic Bluebook 1985: External Economic Relations](https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/other/bluebook/1985/1985-3-1.htm): procurement/repair orders, investment, exports and 1968 second-largest **free-market** economy. The script uses “noncommunist world” to preserve the historical comparison rather than silently applying a modern PPP GDP ranking.
- [National Archives of Japan, High Economic Growth and Income Doubling](https://www.archives.go.jp/learning/archive_collection_8/): workers, development and the 1968 capitalist-economy comparison.

No claim that losing caused prosperity, that occupation alone explains growth, or that economic recovery cancels wartime responsibility. Korean War procurement is shown with an explicit reminder of devastation in Korea.

### Independence, Britain and limits of the comparison

- [Bank of England archive, American loan negotiations](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=1A49&src=CalmView.Catalog): Britain’s external debts and the 1945 loan negotiations.

- [Office of the Historian, Decolonization of Asia and Africa](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/asia-and-africa): war, weakened empires, independence movements and international change. This is one US institutional perspective, not a substitute for histories of individual movements.
- [UK Parliament, Raj and Independence 1914–47](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/the-raj-and-independence-1914-47/): long-running nationalist movements and 1947 independence.
- [UK Parliament, Indian Independence Act 1947](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/collections1/collections2/1947-indian-independence-act/): India/Pakistan, partition and displacement.
- [UK National Army Museum, Independence and Partition](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/independence-and-partition-1947): sovereignty and human cost.
- [National WWII Museum, The Cost of Victory](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/cost-victory): British financial/economic pressure and the uneven postwar position.

India and Pakistan are a contextual example of sovereignty, not additions to a six-country economic ranking. No suggestion that independence was simply bestowed by war, that colonial rule ended immediately everywhere, or that newly independent states were immediate economic winners. Other possible cases — neutral states, other Allied states, China, additional new states — are outside this selected comparison, not declared non-beneficiaries.

## Visual evidence and assets

They have no political borders, no quantitative areas of influence, and no claim to reproduce 1945 boundaries. Backgrounds and thumbnail are AI-generated illustrations; characters, diagrams and period props are original code drawings.
